The problem

Teachers across Jordan and the wider Arab world spend hours each week on lesson planning, quiz creation, rubric design, worksheet generation, and parent communication — work that's repetitive, time-consuming, and disconnected from official curriculum standards. Existing AI education tools (like MagicSchool) are built for English-first, US-curriculum classrooms and don't serve Arabic-speaking teachers or align with national curricula like Jordan's NCCD framework.

The solution

Iqra is a "teacher operating system" — not a learning management system — that gives teachers an AI co-pilot for the full scope of their instructional workload: lesson planning, quiz and worksheet generation, rubric creation, smart reports, and parent communication, all grounded in official curriculum content rather than generic AI output.

What makes it different

  • Arabic-first, not Arabic-translated. Built natively for Arabic language and RTL workflows, not adapted from an English product.
  • Curriculum-grounded by design. Content is generated from Jordan's official NCCD Grade 10 math curriculum (digitized into structured source-of-truth data), not free-form AI generation. The product enforces a strict rule: never present ungrounded content as grounded — UI badges honestly signal which content is fully curriculum-validated versus still in progress.
  • Teacher-led go-to-market. Individual teacher subscriptions (B2C) come first, building trust and usage before schools adopt institutional (B2B) packages — students are a planned Phase 2.

Go-to-market strategy

  • Teachers first — low-friction individual subscriptions build a user base and product feedback loop.
  • Schools follow — seat-based institutional packages once teacher adoption proves value.
  • Students later — a natural Phase 2 expansion once the teacher and school layers are established.
  • Backed by the founder's direct relationships with 18 private schools in Jordan, providing a credible early distribution channel.

Vision

Expand from Jordan into the GCC and broader MENA region, becoming the Arabic-first standard for AI-powered teacher productivity — with curriculum depth, not model ownership, as the long-term moat.
